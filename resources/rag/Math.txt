MATH 113 - Explorations with Data: An Introduction to Data Science and Analytics
This course will provide an introduction to the main ideas of data science and data analytics and their use in a variety of applications. Students will learn how to ask good questions about data and how to present results of data analysis clearly when writing or speaking. Students will be introduced to methods of summarizing and displaying data using spreadsheet-based analytical tools. The course will also include discussion of ethical issues in data collection, storage and analysis. Students will become familiar with career options involving data science and opportunities for further study.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Spring
Attributes: Mathematics (MA)

MATH 115 - Introductory Statistics
The course introduces statistical inference (including confidence interval estimation) for one and two variables using simulation-based methods and then shows how traditional theory-based methods like t-tests, chi-square tests, one-way analysis of variance can also be used. Basic descriptive statistical techniques are reviewed throughout the course and other descriptive techniques such as simple linear regression are introduced. Throughout the course there is an emphasis on conceptual understanding, the entire statistical investigation process, and active learning using group activities and projects, as well as reading and critiquing published research. Cannot receive credit for both Math 115 and MATH 219.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall, Spring
Attributes: Mathematics (MA)

MATH 215 - Intermediate Statistics
This course provides a survey of statistical methods students would expect to see utilized across disciplines in peer reviewed research. Students focus on potential sources of variability, study design, and analyses of studies where the research questions involve more than two variables simultaneously. Topics include multiple regression, non-parametric methods, generalized linear models, and multivariate statistical models. The course culminates in a final project where students design their own study, collect & analyze data using statistical software, and present their results. Cannot receive credit for both MATH 215 and MATH 219. MATH 115 or previous statistics experience is highly recommended prior to this course.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall, Spring
Attributes: Mathematics (MA)

MATH 219 - Statistical Methods
This course provides a survey of statistical methods students would expect to see utilized across disciplines in peer reviewed research. Topics include descriptive statistics, data visualization, statistical inference for one or more variables, parameter estimation using confidence intervals, understanding sources of variability, and study design. Students are introduced to both simulation-based and traditional theory-based inference. There is an emphasis on conceptual understanding and active learning using group activities and projects. The statistical methods covered in this course are used in peer-reviewed research across a variety of disciplines, and students will be introduced to statistical software that is widely-used in these fields. Students cannot receive credit for both MATH 115/MATH 215 and MATH 219.

Credits: 4
Terms Offered: Fall, Spring
Attributes: Mathematics (MA)

MATH 221 - Introduction to Mathematics for Elementary Educators

MATH 321 - History of Mathematics
This course is designed to give mathematics students an opportunity to study the various periods of mathematical development. Attention will be given to the early Egyptian-Babylonian period, the geometry of Greek mathematicians, the Hindu and Arabian contribution, the evolvement of analytical geometry since Descartes, the development of calculus by Newton and Leibniz, and non-Euclidean geometry. Some attention will be given to the methods and symbolisms used in problem solving during various periods of time.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall
Prerequisites: MATH 132 with a grade of C- or better
