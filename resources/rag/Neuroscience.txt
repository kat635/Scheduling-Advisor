NSCI 211 - Introduction to Neuroscience
This interdisciplinary course covers basic information from biology, chemistry, psychology, and philosophy that is relevant for understanding the nervous system and its role in behavior. Topics include structure and function of neurons, brain anatomy, sensory and motor systems, and the neuroscience of motivation, emotion, sleep, memory, language, and consciousness.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall, Spring
Attributes: Natural Science with Lab (NSL)
Corequisites: NSCI 211L

NSCI 211L - Introduction to Neuroscience Laboratory
This is the laboratory that accompanies NSCI 211. Laboratory projects expose students to research methods in neuroscience, including monitoring the activity of individual neurons and recording physiological responses from humans.

Credits: 1
Terms Offered: Fall, Spring
Attributes: Natural Science with Lab (NSL)
Corequisites: NSCI 211

NSCI 312 - Functional Neuroanatomy
This course offers an in-depth exploration of the structure and function of the vertebrate nervous system. After a review of neuroanatomy principles and the organization of the central and peripheral nervous systems, students will explore the anatomical and functional bases of sensory systems, motor control, memory, reward, and emotion. This course will use clinical cases in order to emphasize how the fundamental neuroanatomy principles relate to human nervous system function in health and disease.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall
Prerequisites: Sophomore standing. NSCI 211; or, BIOL 105 and BIOL 106, BIOL 107 or equivalent, BIOL 108 or equivalent
Corequisites: NSCI 312L 

NSCI 312L - Functional Neuroanatomy Laboratory
This course complements NSCI 312 lecture and offers further exploration of the structure and function of the vertebrate nervous system. Students study neuroanatomy using dissections of fixed brain specimens and histology slides for light microscopy. Students also learn fundamental experimental techniques such as preparation of brain specimens for immunohistochemical analysis, light microscopy and confocal microscopy. Students enhance their understanding of the tridimensional nature of neuroanatomical structures by building neuroanatomical models.

Credits: 1
Terms Offered: Fall
Prerequisites: Sophomore standing, NSCI 211 
Corequisites: NSCI 312 

NSCI 313 - Neurophysiology
This course is focused on the neurophysiological function of the nervous system, ranging from cellular neurophysiology to the physiology of neural circuits and systems that underlie sensation, cognition, and behavior. The course will begin with a foundation in the core concepts of cellular neurophysiology, emphasizing the principles underlying resting membrane potential, action potentials, the physiology of voltage gated and synaptic ion channels, and synaptic integration and plasticity. The course will then explore the physiology of neural circuits and their relationship to behavior.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Spring
Prerequisites: Declared neuroscience major or minor, Sophomore standing, NSCI 211, NSCI 211L 
Corequisites: NSCI 313L

NSCI 335 - Neurochemistry and Disease
In this course, students will explore how the biochemistry of the brain influences nervous system function, specifically in relationship to motor and cognitive processes. Students will initially be introduced to fundamental aspects of neuroscience and biochemistry in a traditional lecture format. After this introduction, students will explore the relationship between altered neurochemical activity and disease states using a case study approach. In the lab, students will be introduced to several neurochemistry techniques and will then be asked to use these tools to complete a novel neurochemistry research project. The NSCI 335L laboratory is optional for all other students taking the course. Cross-listed with BIOL 335 and CHEM 335.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Spring
Prerequisites: BIOL 105 and BIOL 106, BIOL 107 or equivalent, BIOL 108 or equivalent; or, CHEM 311 or NSCI 211

NSCI 411 - Advanced Neuroscience Research I
An interdisciplinary course in which neuroscience majors and minors work as a team to complete a novel neuroscience research project supervised by the instructor. Students will read and discuss primary research literature, write an independent research proposal, design and conduct a neuroscience research project, and write a formal research manuscript summarizing their research findings.

Terms Offered: Fall
Prerequisites: Declared neuroscience major or minor, Senior standing, NSCI 211
