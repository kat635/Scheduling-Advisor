NURS 210 - Introduction to Professional Nursing
This course introduces professional nursing within the context of the Christian faith. The course content includes nursing theory, critical thinking, professional nursing roles, community-based nursing, nursing process and holistic nursing care for individuals, families, groups and communities across cultures. Open to non-nursing majors by permission of the chairperson.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall, Spring
Prerequisites: Declared nursing major or permission of chairperson

NURS 232 - Nursing Skills Laboratory
This course will develop introductory healthcare and professional nursing psychomotor skills through laboratory practice. Skills will be examined with a focus on the provision of introductory evidence-based care to individuals across the lifespan and across cultures. This course is a combination of theory and applied psychomotor skills in the laboratory. Theory, one hour per week: Laboratory three hours per week and three hours per week of practice/independent study. Students may take KIN 200 either prior to enrollment in or concurrently with the class.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall, Spring
Prerequisites: Declared nursing major, KIN 200 
Corequisites: KIN 200 

NURS 255 - Health Assessment
This course presents the process of health assessment for individuals, families, groups, and communities. Physical, psychological, social, spiritual, and cultural assessment skills and techniques are developed, focusing on lifespan application within the context of the Christian faith. This course is a combination of theory and applied experiences in the laboratory and community. Theory, one hour per week; Laboratory/Community, three hours per week. BIOL 221, KIN 200, and PSY 100 may be taken either prior to enrollment in or concurrently with the class.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall, Spring
Prerequisites: Declared nursing major or permission of chairperson, BIOL 221 with a grade of C or better, KIN 200 with a grade of C or better, PSY 100 with a grade of C or better
Corequisites: BIOL 221, KIN 200, PSY 100 

NURS 260 - Pharmacology
This course provides a study of medication and their interactions with individuals across the lifespan and across cultures. A focus will be placed on roles of the professional nurse and other health professionals in using a problem solving process for the promotion of optimal health. Open to non-nursing majors by permission of the chairperson. NURS 310 - Nursing Care Across the Lifespan I
This course will explore topics common within professional nursing practice including lab/diagnostic testing, genetics, care of patients with chronic illnesses, interprofessional collaboration, group process and family theories. NURS 390 - Enhancing Self-Care and Well-Being for Nursing Students
This elective course will focus on enhancing the self-care practices and well-being of nursing students. The course will introduce students to self-care theory and the dimensions of self-care including physical, intellectual, social, emotional, spiritual, vocational, financial, and environmental self-care. Students will explore key concepts for each self-care dimension expanding their understanding of self-care and actively participating in self-care practices during and outside of class. Students will journal throughout the semester to reflect on their learning and self-care practices. Students will write a research paper examining the evidence for one self-care practice. Throughout the course, students will work on creating a self-care plan unique to their individual self-care goals and needs and present the plan at the end of the semester. Completion of one theory and practicum course is required prior to enrollment in or concurrently with the class.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall
Prerequisites: Declared nursing major, Junior or senior standing, NURS 210 with a grade of C or better

NURS 470 - Clinical Judgment in Nursing
This course provides a comprehensive evaluation of clinical judgment in nursing and analyzes advanced concepts of caring for the complex, high acuity adult patient across cultures and lifespan in a variety of healthcare settings. Emphasis will be placed on exploring the provision of evidenced-based nursing care for adults with multisystem dysfunction to promote or restore optimal health. An emphasis will also be placed on the critical evaluation of specialty content areas, with a focus on the integration of critical thinking skills necessary for providing evidence-based care to patients. This course also includes in-depth preparation for the National Council Licensure Examination for Registered Nurses (NCLEX-RN). Students must reach a benchmarked score on an NCLEX-RN practice exam (4 hours in length) or complete remediation work as part of this course. Students may take NURS 481 and NURS 488 either prior to enrollment in or concurrently with the class.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall, Spring
Prerequisites: Declared nursing major, NURS 481 with a grade of C or better, NURS 488 with a grade of C or better
Corequisites: NURS 481, NURS 488 

NURS 481 - Nursing Management and Transitions
This course provides an in-depth examination of issues essential to nursing leadership and management roles and professional nursing practice. An emphasis will be placed on integration of critical thinking skills and management skills necessary for providing evidence-based care for patients across the lifespan and across cultures in a variety of health care settings. This course will provide an opportunity to evaluate post graduate professional opportunities for graduate nursing education and advanced nurse roles. The course will focus on evaluating the professional nurse roles of research team member, utilizer of research, educator, advanced care provider, collaborator, designer of care, manager/leader, advocate for patients, member and advocate of the nursing profession, and life-long learner. Completion of three theory and practicum courses is required prior to this course.

Credits: 3
Terms Offered: Fall
Prerequisites: Declared nursing major

NURS 488 - Nursing Internship
This internship, supervised by the Department of Nursing, is done in cooperation with a health care agency. Students will select an area of clinical interest to apply previously acquired knowledge and to develop competencies and skills necessary for the roles of the professional nurse in the provision of evidence-based care. The student will have a minimum of 120 hours of clinical experience while being mentored by a professional nurse. This half-semester course meets both first half and last half of each semester.
